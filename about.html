Biography

Isaac “Ollie” Oloche Johnson is a Nigerian-born visual artist, creative technologist and cultural researcher, known for work that moves between painting, drawing, digital printmaking, collaborative installation and archival research. His practice attends to memory, ritual, ecological loss and the afterlives of cultural heritage. Born in Nigeria, Johnson studied social psychology and is based in Aberdeen, United Kingdom. In 2024 he was a Dreaming New Worlds Fellow at Goethe-Institut Nigeria, Lagos, where, with the Chronoverse collective (Olúbùnmi Atere, Olorunyemi Kolapo Obadiah and Johnson), he developed the installation Totem, an investigation of rites of passage, the Igba boi apprenticeship system of Eastern Nigeria and the material representation of totems across human societies. The work was presented in Kedu Lagos, curated by Chinyere Obieze, at the Old Government Printing Press, Lagos (2–30 November 2024), where Johnson also gave an artist talk

In 2025 Johnson took part in the fifth annual GroundWork artists’ residency at GroundWork Gallery, King’s Lynn, Norfolk, producing the Fluid Earth series, three digital prints Irregular Hourglass, When Water Wears Masks and Tears of the Creek that examine oil pollution in the Niger Delta. The series was shown in the group exhibition Fluid Earth at GroundWork Gallery (11 June–14 December 2025).

Johnson’s work with technology and archives includes a 2024 role as Assistant Digital Archivist at the Museum of West African Art (MOWAA), Benin City, and talks on technology and African art at Pan-Atlantic University, Lagos, and Akure Tech Hub, Akure. Since 2025 he has been developing The Traveller’s Archive, a transnational artistic-research project housed at the Centre for African Studies Basel (ZASB) that works with audiovisual archives in Switzerland and Nigeria, beginning with Hans Hürzeler’s film reels of the 1940s and 1950s. Alongside these projects he continues a studio practice in painting and drawing. Johnson lives and works in Aberdeen.
