Project · Residency · 2025

Fluid
Earth

Three digital prints developed through the fifth annual GroundWork artists’ residency, addressing oil pollution, water, time and the Niger Delta.

Completed project

The three works — Irregular Hourglass, When Water Wears Masks and Tears of the Creek — formed part of GroundWork Gallery’s 2025 Fluid Earth exhibition in King’s Lynn, Norfolk.

The exhibition brought together artists responding to changing relationships between humans, non-humans and the earth. Johnson’s contribution connects the environmental language of the residency to the oil-affected landscape of the Niger Delta.

Context

Irregular Hourglass

The work begins from an oil-spill photograph whose reflection suggested an irregular hourglass. Johnson transforms that image into a symbolic structure in which crude oil replaces sand, shifting the hourglass from a measure of time toward a measure of ecological loss.

GroundWork lists the work as a digital art print, 60 × 14 cm, and notes that proceeds from its prints supported a Niger Delta community connected to the project.
